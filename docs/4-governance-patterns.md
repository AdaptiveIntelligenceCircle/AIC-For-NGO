# 04 – Lightweight Governance Patterns

These patterns are offered for reflection only.  
They are not operational prescriptions.

## 1. Decision integrity log

For decisions above a certain threshold of significance (funding, formal alliances, major public positions, large program changes):

- Record the date, options considered, key reasons, participants, and any persistent dissent.
- Keep the log internal if necessary, but make the *practice* of logging non-negotiable.

## 2. Explicit human authorization points

Define categories of decisions that require named human authorization and cannot be fully delegated to staff urgency, donor timelines, or automated recommendations.

## 3. Periodic capture review

At set intervals, examine:

- Which external relationships currently exert the most influence on strategy?
- Have any of those relationships begun to shape what the organization is willing to say or do?
- What is the realistic cost of reducing dependence on the most influential sources of support?

## 4. Separation of development and authorization

Where feasible, separate the work of developing proposals from the final authority to approve them.  
This creates a structural pause.

## 5. Restraint in public association

Any public reference to AIC or AIC-For-NGO should accurately reflect the limited, experimental, and non-endorsing nature of the relationship.
