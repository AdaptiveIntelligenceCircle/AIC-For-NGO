# 02 – Principles Mapping for NGO Contexts

## 1. Ethical-from-kernel

Critical organizational decisions (major funding acceptance, formal partnerships, public advocacy positions, significant program shifts) benefit from having an explicit evaluation moment before final authorization.  

The default posture when alignment with independence or mission is unclear should lean toward pause or refusal rather than retrospective justification.

## 2. Third Path in contested environments

NGOs frequently sit between state power, large philanthropic/commercial funders, and political movements.  

Third Path thinking here means:

- Regularly examining whether any relationship has begun to function as de facto control
- Preferring structures that preserve the practical ability to say “no”
- Avoiding arrangements that make organizational survival dependent on continuous alignment with one powerful external agenda

## 3. Non-capture discipline

Funding is often necessary. The discipline lies in noticing when funding begins to rewrite priorities, silence certain analyses, or create personal/institutional dependencies that distort judgment.  

Transparency about major funding conditions is treated as a governance requirement, not optional communications.

## 4. Human Meaning

In high-pressure advocacy environments, there is strong temptation to let strategy, metrics, and external expectations replace deeper questions of purpose.  

AIC-For-NGO encourages protecting deliberate human space for those questions, especially when the organization is under time or funding pressure.

## 5. Under-claim and restraint

Because NGOs often operate in polarized contexts, any association with AIC must remain modest.  

Over-claiming usefulness or alignment risks both the NGO and the integrity of AIC’s broader stance.
