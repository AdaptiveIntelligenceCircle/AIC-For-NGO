# 03 – Eligibility Reflection & Heightened Caution

This is not a certification process.  
It is a set of reflective questions and caution flags specific to the higher-risk environment in which many NGOs operate.

## Core reflective questions

1. Can the organization still refuse significant funding or partnership offers without existential threat?
2. Are major advocacy or positioning decisions made with clear internal deliberation, or primarily driven by donor expectations and media cycles?
3. Is there willingness to document the reasoning behind significant strategic choices?
4. Does leadership treat the risk of capture (by funders, governments, or political alignments) as a live concern?
5. Is the interest in AIC principles driven by a desire for deeper integrity, or primarily by branding / legitimacy needs?

## Heightened caution flags

- Heavy reliance on a small number of large institutional funders with strong agenda preferences
- Operating model that requires continuous public campaigning intensity
- History of rapid shifts in program focus following funding opportunities
- Desire for public association with AIC before internal practice is established
- Expectation that AIC will provide cover, legitimacy, or technical services

## Stance for AIC maintainers

Documentation in this repository may be read and adapted by any organization.  

However, deeper collaboration, joint statements, or technical integration should be approached with significantly higher caution than in the AIC-For-NPO context.  
When multiple caution flags are present, the default posture is restraint.
